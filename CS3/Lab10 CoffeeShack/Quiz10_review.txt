ANSWER KEY AND EXPLANATIONS
===========================

1.  C - The old pointer is passed to Sugar's Ingredient base and stored as drink_.
        The caller's pointer now refers to the outermost wrapper. Earlier drinks
        and decorators remain inside the chain and are not immediately deleted.
2.  A - Medium costs 7; sugar adds 1, cream 2, and the second sugar another 1.
        Descriptions recurse inward first, preserving entry order and repeats.
3.  D - main constructs Manager manager, SeniorBarista senior(&manager), and
        JuniorBarista junior(&senior). serveCustomer() begins prepare() at junior.
4.  B - A plain drink's description has no " with " marker. Junior handles it
        regardless of cup size; takeOrder() sets a large coffee's base price to 10.
5.  C - Honey adds 3 to the large base price of 10. Junior delegates because
        ingredients exist, and Senior delegates because the description has honey.
6.  A - notifyCustomers() loops over the entire waiting list. Each update()
        compares ready with this, so only the matching object prints a response.
7.  D - Two people named Alex can be distinct Customer objects. Object identity
        prevents both from claiming a drink merely because their names match.
8.  B - Customer deletes its outer Drink*. Drink's virtual destructor reaches
        Ingredient's destructor, which deletes the next wrapped Drink recursively.
9.  C - The selected waiting pointer is customers_[rand() % customers_.size()].
        Service is not restricted to the vector's front and does not use threads.
10. A - nullptr breaks the order-taking loop, but execution continues into the
        final while(junior.hasCustomers()) loop before final object cleanup.
11. D - The constructor defaults to DrinkType::small and price 0. Prices 5, 7,
        and 10 are explicitly supplied by takeOrder(), not inferred by Drink.
12. B - Ingredient::getName() appends " with " for the first ingredient and
        ", " thereafter. Each outer decorator appends its own ingredient last.
13. C - Both orders add 1 + 2 to the small price 5. The description follows
        wrapper construction order, so sugar/cream and cream/sugar are distinct.
14. A - Every h creates another Honey wrapper. No deduplication occurs, so
        10 + 3 + 3 = 16 and both honey additions appear in the description.
15. D - The junior uses name.find(" with ") == string::npos to recognize an
        undecorated drink. Size and price are not its routing criteria.
16. B - Senior searches for the substring "honey" anywhere in the description.
        Other ingredients or their order do not prevent forwarding to Manager.
17. C - The size switch has only lowercase l, m, and s cases. An invalid token
        leaves drink null, so the size-selection loop requests another choice.
18. A - Earlier char extraction leaves whitespace, including newlines. std::ws
        consumes leading whitespace; getline then reads the rest of the name's
        line, allowing names such as Alex Smith rather than stopping at a space.
19. D - The failed ingredient extraction deletes drink and returns nullptr.
        Recursive decorator destruction frees the partial order. main registers
        a customer only when takeOrder() returns a non-null Customer*.
20. B - main's vector retains all accepted customers. The junior's separate
        vector tracks only waiting observers. Final deletion occurs through
        main's vector, even for customers already removed from the waiting list.
21. C - The method erases a matching pointer entry and returns. It does not
        delete the pointee; main remains responsible for Customer destruction.
22. A - The ready customer needs to receive update(ready) before its pointer is
        removed from the observer list. Other waiting customers receive it too.
23. D - With no waiting customer there is nothing to serve. The early return
        also prevents using zero as the modulus and indexing an empty vector.
24. B - manager, senior, and junior are automatic variables in main, declared
        in an order that keeps the linked handlers alive while serving. next_
        does not own its target; Barista's destructor does not delete it.
25. C - The for loop runs at most three order-taking iterations and stops early
        if takeOrder() returns nullptr. Remaining accepted orders still finish.
26. A - Waiting orders coexist as registered customers. The single-threaded
        program interleaves taking orders and serving randomly selected customers.
27. D - main registers customers only with junior. prepare() forwarding does
        not call registerCustomer() on senior or manager; junior still announces,
        notifies, and unregisters the ready order after preparation is selected.
28. B - The virtual call reaches Sugar::getPrice(). It delegates to the stored
        inner drink and adds 1, rather than reading the wrapper's inherited price_.
29. C - The variable choice is interpreted in different stages of takeOrder().
        Its character value has meaning according to the switch that handles it.
30. A - Alex: 5. Brenda: 7 + 1 + 2 + 1 = 11. Chris: 10 + 3 + 3 = 16.
        The total is 5 + 11 + 16 = 32; random scheduling changes service order,
        not the ingredients or charges of accepted orders.
